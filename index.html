M.Sc. student in Language Science and Technology · Saarland University

Hi, I’m Sylvia 🌲

I’m a master’s student in Language Science and Technology at Saarland University. My research aims to understand how humans and language models reason about other minds when they communicate.

  • What does it take to represent another agent’s beliefs, and to tell shared knowledge apart from what only one side knows?
  • How do these representations change as a conversation unfolds?
  • Where do the apparent similarities between language models and human social cognition break down?

I approach these questions by combining computational experiments with language models, ideas from pragmatics and cognitive science, and, where possible, comparisons with human data. I care about what a test actually measures, and about results that can tell competing explanations apart, not only about whether a model gets the answer right.

I hope this work helps us understand human communication better, and build language models that communicate in ways people can rely on.

Research Interests

Computational linguistics, psycholinguistics, pragmatics, social cognition, artificial intelligence

Current focus

  • Theory of Mind and social cognition in language models
  • Computational pragmatics: common ground, implicature, Gricean and rational-speech-act reasoning
  • Belief and information-state updating in discourse and in multi-agent settings
  • Human–model comparison: behavioral versus mechanistic similarity

Before Saarland, I studied English at Southwest University in Chongqing.
